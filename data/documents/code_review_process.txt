SPOONFUL, INC. — ENGINEERING CODE REVIEW PROCESS
Document owner: VP of Engineering
Classification: Internal
Last reviewed: November 2025

OVERVIEW
Code review is a mandatory quality gate at Spoonful. No code reaches the main
branch or production without review and approval. The process exists to catch
defects early, share knowledge across the team, and maintain a consistent,
secure codebase.

PULL REQUEST REQUIREMENTS
All changes are submitted as a pull request (PR) against the main branch. A PR
must include a clear description of what changed and why, a link to the related
ticket, and evidence that automated tests pass. PRs should be small and focused;
the target is under 400 lines of changed code. Large PRs are returned to the
author to be split.

REVIEWER ASSIGNMENT
Every PR requires at least one approving review from an engineer other than the
author. Changes to security-sensitive areas — authentication, authorization,
payment handling, or data encryption — require two approvals, one of which must
come from a senior engineer. The CODEOWNERS file determines required reviewers
for protected directories.

WHAT REVIEWERS CHECK
Reviewers assess correctness, readability, test coverage, and security.
Reviewers confirm that new logic has tests, that error cases are handled, that
no secrets or credentials are committed, and that the change does not introduce
known vulnerable dependencies. Style issues are handled by automated linters,
not manual review, so reviewers focus on substance.

SERVICE-LEVEL EXPECTATIONS
Reviewers are expected to provide first feedback within one business day.
Authors are expected to respond to review comments within one business day.
Blocking disagreements are escalated to the team lead rather than left to stall.

MERGING AND DEPLOYMENT
Once approved and all checks pass, the author merges the PR. Deployment to
production occurs through the automated CI/CD pipeline. Hotfixes follow the same
review process but may use an expedited single-senior-approval path with a
retrospective review within 24 hours.

CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT
Review practices are revisited each quarter in the engineering retrospective.
Metrics such as time-to-first-review and defect-escape rate are tracked to
identify bottlenecks.
